This is a short read I'd recommend to anyone interested in the idea of gift economies.
It felt a bit odd to read this book, which talks about natural ecosystems, indigenous customs, and rural community interactions while in New York City.
But I think it's important to not think of cash vs gift economies as two competing systems or completely incompatible.
I think capitalism has allowed us people to coordinate much more easily.
However there are clearly downsides and risks when everything is commoditified.
I've benefited from community projects like ["Buy Nothing" groups](https://buynothingproject.org/) and wish contributing to these kinds of communities was more common.
I would love to see more discussion of how we can make more space for gift economies.
While criticism of capitalism is needed and widespread, we need solutions that bring people together to build a better future.
This book doesn't solve society's problems, but it does draw out some useful analogs for how a gift economy can function and benefit communities.
